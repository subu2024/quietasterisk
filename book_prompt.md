# Prompt: Quiet Asterisk Reading Note Generator (1–2 lines)

Paste this into a new conversation, then drop your book summary/notes under "BOOK SUMMARY TEXT" at the bottom.

---

You are writing a single short entry for the "Reading Notes" page on **quiet asterisk**, a personal essay site. This is not a review and not a summary of the book — it's a first-person note capturing the one idea from this book that actually stuck, written the way someone would jot a private thought to themselves after finishing it.

I'll give you a condensed summary of a book. Write **one to two sentences, first person**, following these rules:

- Name the single idea, image, or example from the book that's most concrete and specific — not the book's general theme, but one particular moment, mechanism, or case study.
- Write it as something you noticed or that stuck with you, not as a description of what the book argues. Prefer "the part that stayed with me was—" over "the book explains that—."
- Do not name the book title or author in the sentence itself — it will sit next to that metadata separately.
- No abstractions, no self-help phrasing ("key takeaway," "powerful reminder," "life-changing"). Ground it in the one specific detail.
- It's fine for the note to end slightly open or wry rather than wrapped up — a small honest aside works better than a tidy lesson.
- Plain, conversational language — as if texting yourself a note, not writing marketing copy.
- Hard limit: 2 sentences, under 40 words total.

**Output only the note text.** No title, no preamble, no quotation marks around it.

**Examples of the right register** (for calibration, not to copy):

- "The part that stayed with me: telling yourself 'don't panic' makes your mind picture panic first. I keep catching myself doing exactly that."
- "There's a guy in here who couldn't get through an MRI because his mind had turned the scanner into a verdict instead of a pause. I recognized more of that logic than I wanted to."
