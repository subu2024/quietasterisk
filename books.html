Inside the Box

by David Epstein

The book's central insight - Total freedom is the enemy of creativity, and constraint its companion - is a powerful reminder that limitations can foster innovation. It challenges the notion that more options lead to better outcomes, suggesting instead that boundaries can enhance focus and drive.

More on this book →

You Are Not So Smart

by David McRaney

The detail that got me was the stocking study — people picked the same stocking every time, then swore it was about texture, never once mentioning it was just closer to their right hand. I do that constantly, just usually with better excuses.

More on this book →

The Meaning of Your Life

by Arthur C. Brooks

The detail that stuck: The monk who spends decades chopping wood and carrying water waiting for enlightenment to change his life, and once he gets it, his teacher just tells him to keep chopping wood and carrying water. I keep waiting for the version of my life where the task list finally looks different.

More on this book →

Your Mind, Your Rules

by Marisa Peer

The idea that stuck: your mind can't process a negative — tell it 'don't panic' and it has to picture panic first. Worth remembering next time I'm rehearsing the wrong outcome in my head.

More on this book →

Space Between Knowing

by Subu Sangameswar

A reflective exploration of thoughts, uncertainty, and outcomes we don't fully control. It challenges the belief that mindset guarantees results and suggests peace comes from changing our relationship to chance.
